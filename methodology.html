What this platform does

ClimateAI Intelligence maps surface heat, vegetation, built-up areas, population, air temperature and rainfall in Islamabad District. It shows how many people live in the hottest areas, summarises the data in charts and tables, and uses machine learning models to predict surface heat, explain what drives it, test what-if scenarios and rank priority areas. It is a screening tool built on one summer season of data. It is not an official risk assessment.

Data

LayerSource and processing
Land surface temperature (LST)Landsat 8 and 9 thermal data, 30 m. Composite of 5 clear scenes: 29 May, 6 Jun, 14 Jun, 30 Jun and 8 Jul 2026.
Vegetation (NDVI) and built-up index (NDBI)Sentinel-2, 30 m grid. Composite of 8 dates between 29 May and 8 Jul 2026.
Built-up probabilityDynamic World built-up probability, averaged over 29 May to 8 Jul 2026.
Population densityWorldPop 2020 gridded estimate, 100 m.
ElevationSRTM digital elevation model (USGS and NASA, SRTMGL1 v003), 30 m, averaged to 100 m.
Air temperatureERA5-Land daily mean, 29 May to 8 Jul 2026. Grid of about 11 km, smoothed for display.
RainfallCHIRPS daily rainfall, summed over the same dates. Grid of about 5 km, smoothed for display.
Study areaIslamabad District, FAO GAUL level 2 boundary (about 895 km²).

All layers were processed in Google Earth Engine and Python in the author's UrbanSense: Twin Cities university project (BS Geoinformatics, PMAS-Arid Agriculture University). The surface layers were resampled to a common 100 m grid (UTM zone 43N) and clipped to Islamabad District, giving 89,090 pixels of 0.01 km² each. Population totals are density multiplied by pixel area. The pixel table behind the charts and models has one row per pixel with longitude, latitude, LST, NDVI, NDBI, built-up probability, elevation, population density and exposure class.

Heat exposure classes

ClassDefinition
Other areasPixels that are not in the top 25% of land surface temperature.
Hot (top 25% LST)Land surface temperature at or above the 75th percentile, but not meeting the hotspot rule below.
HotspotLand surface temperature at or above the 75th percentile, and NDVI at or below the 25th percentile (sparse vegetation), and population density at or above the 75th percentile.

The thresholds are percentiles of the whole Twin Cities study area (Rawalpindi and Islamabad districts). They are relative to this region and are not health standards. Because Islamabad is cooler on average than Rawalpindi, a smaller share of Islamabad falls into the hot classes.

Charts and tables

  • Distributions: each layer is shown as a histogram with 30 bins, with the mean, median, standard deviation, 5th and 95th percentiles, minimum and maximum.
  • Relationships: pixels are grouped into 12 bins of each variable (NDVI, NDBI, built-up probability and elevation), and the mean land surface temperature of each bin is plotted.
  • Correlation: Pearson correlation between all six surface variables. Land surface temperature is most strongly related to NDBI (r = 0.82) and NDVI (r = −0.81), then elevation (r = −0.65), built-up probability (r = 0.43) and population density (r = 0.19). NDVI and NDBI are strongly related to each other (r = −0.91).
  • Class comparison: the table above compares the three exposure classes.

AI models

All models predict land surface temperature from five inputs: NDVI, NDBI, built-up probability, elevation and population density. Air temperature and rainfall are shown on the map but are not model inputs.

ModelTest R²RMSEMAE
Linear regression0.7771.60 °C1.28 °C
Random forest0.8171.45 °C1.15 °C
Gradient boosting0.8161.45 °C1.17 °C

Each model was trained on 66,446 pixels and tested on the other 22,644 of the 89,090 pixels. The nonlinear models fit the data better than the linear model, and random forest and gradient boosting perform almost the same.

  • Dashboard risk model: Random Forest regression on the 100 m grid. The area was split into blocks of about 2 km, and 25% of the blocks were held out for testing. The model never saw the test areas during training. Test R² is 0.83 and the error is 1.39 °C.
  • Importance: permutation importance measured on the held-out blocks. Built-up index (NDBI) ranks first (40%), then vegetation (29%) and elevation (26%). Built-up probability and population density add little (about 3% each). The Explainable AI workspace gives the same ranking of the top three.
  • Partial dependence: the Explainable AI workspace also draws curves showing how the predicted temperature changes as one input moves across its range while the others are held at their typical values.
  • Prediction workspace: compares the three models for a chosen place or custom inputs, with ready-made profiles for the Islamabad average, the hotspot average and a green area (top 10% NDVI).
  • What-if scenarios (dashboard and Simulator): the model is re-run with NDVI raised by a fixed amount, or with vegetation or built-up area changed, while the other inputs stay the same. For NDVI raised by 0.05, 0.10 and 0.15 everywhere, the modelled mean surface temperature falls from 40.45 °C to 40.29, 40.03 and 39.71 °C. This is a model-based estimate of the average change, not a forecast.

Risk intelligence and priority areas

Islamabad is split into cells of about 1 km. Each cell is scored from 0 to 100 as the average of three ranks: hotter surface, less vegetation and more people. The top 10 cells are listed as priority areas. For each cell the cooling value is the drop in mean surface temperature that the random forest model estimates if NDVI rose by 0.10 there. It is a model scenario, not a forecast. The Risk Intelligence workspace also lets you build a weighted heat-risk map by toggling layers.

AI Climate Copilot

The Copilot answers questions using this project's own data and model results. Its answers are only as complete as the data on this page and should be read with the limitations below.

Limitations

  • The data covers one summer season (May to July 2026). The models explain spatial patterns of heat and cannot forecast future temperatures or show trends.
  • Land surface temperature is the temperature of the ground and roofs seen by the satellite. It is not air temperature.
  • Air temperature (about 11 km) and rainfall (about 5 km) are coarse and smoothed. They show regional patterns, not differences between neighbourhoods.
  • The models find associations, not causes. Elevation mostly reflects that higher ground is cooler, which cannot be changed. NDVI and NDBI are strongly related, so their importance shares overlap.
  • WorldPop is a modelled estimate for 2020, so population counts are approximate.
  • Results have not yet been checked against ground measurements such as weather stations.
  • The models were trained and tested inside Islamabad District only and should not be applied to other regions without retraining.
